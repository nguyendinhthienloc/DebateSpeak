# Vision Document drafts: Lộc (B-5)

### FG-08 AI Fact-Checking & Evidence

Spoken debates reward confident delivery, so an invented statistic can win a round that it should lose. FG-08 lets a learner see which of their factual claims held up: a Fact Investigator picks out check-worthy claims from the transcript, searches trusted sources, and an Evidence Evaluator returns one of five verdicts (Supported, Partially Supported, Contradicted, Insufficient Evidence, Not Verifiable) with source links. We show uncertainty openly instead of forcing a yes or no, because a wrong "Contradicted" on a correct claim would damage the learner's trust more than a plain "Insufficient Evidence". Each verdict quotes the exact words the speaker used, so the learner can see what was judged. This turns the habit of asserting facts into the habit of backing them, which is also the most transferable skill a debate gives an English learner.

### FG-10 Learning History & Reports

A single ten-minute debate produces a lot of feedback, and most of it is forgotten by the next session. FG-10 combines language metrics, argument structure, fact-check verdicts and judge scores into one post-debate report, then ends it with a short practice plan tied to quoted moments from the transcript. Over time it keeps a history per learner, so the learner can see whether the same weakness, such as dropped articles or unanswered rebuttals, appears in debate after debate. The report states weak results plainly and pairs each one with a concrete exercise, so it informs without discouraging. This is what makes DebateSpeak a learning tool across weeks instead of a one-off game.
